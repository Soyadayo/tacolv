<u>A sculpture at the center, a few trees in the square, people</u>

People have gathered in a circle around the sculpture at the center. They are busy chatting. But suddenly, silence falls. There is only a premonition that something is about to begin. The people gradually lower their voices, turning their eyes toward the sculpture at the center.

Silence.

From the tops of the trees planted around the sculpture, sounds descend. Bb, F, Bb, D, F. When the sounds ring out, the people begin talking again, as if they understood that this was not what was supposed to begin.

A man appears

The man circles around the sculpture, tracing a circle with it at the center. He raises his right hand, gripping it as if holding a bus strap, occasionally seeming to say something, but the people cannot make it out. The man moves in small steps, bumping into the sculpture as he circles.

The people watched the man while continuing to chat. No one was captivated. They simply could not look away. Not because he was fascinating.

Eventually the man slowed down, slipped into the ring of people, and disappeared.

<u>A sculpture at the center of the square, about a dozen cats around it, three people smoking on a bench in the back</u>

Three people sit on the bench in the back. All three are smoking. Whether they know each other is unclear. They simply sit on the same bench, exhaling smoke in the same direction.

From the right, the smell of cigarettes comes toward you in a clump. The smell appears and disappears. The shape the smell takes in the air resembles a bubble. Round.

The moment the outline of that bubble forms a clear image in your mind, the cats begin to cry. Not just one. All of them. At first, separately. Each one crying at its own pitch, its own length. But after a few repetitions, the outlines of their cries begin to resemble one another. As if they are trying to produce the same sound. They are approaching a single note, but the precision is low. Low, yet something like intention is there.

The three on the bench put out their cigarettes, as if by agreement.

The cats' voices diminish. One by one.

In the distance, a motorbike engine starts.

The last cat stops crying.

Philopappos Hill
37.967263132340506, 23.72163957791652

<u>A man stands on top of the hill, strong wind, holding a kite</u>

This man's son was born three months ago. He started making the kite three months before that. Around the time his wife's belly had finally begun to visibly swell, the man spread his materials in a corner of his workshop. The type of bamboo for the frame. The thickness of the paper. The mixture of the glue. And what to paint on the surface. Everything was decided from the first day. It was less a plan than a confirmation by hand of something that had been there long before.

No one told the man to do it, yet somehow he believed it would be for the sake of the child to come, and he began. His wife and his colleagues did not stop him. They did not find it strange. When the man was silently making something, there was no room to interject. And so, the kite was completed. The kite, three meters square, bore the child's name and a sky full of stars. The man told his wife he had reserved a table at the restaurant at the bottom of the hill. That was all he said. He said nothing about the kite.

In the evening, his wife and child were at the restaurant.

The man stood on top of the hill with ten companions and raised the kite.

The kite rose into the sky. Powerfully, straight up.

But the man had made one mistake.

For six months, he had never once thought about the spatial relationship between the top and the bottom of the hill. The sky full of stars and the name were painted on the front of the kite. The front faces upward, toward the man's side.

What his wife was looking up at was the blank white back, with nothing painted on it.

The man has not noticed.

<u>Inside the restaurant, a round table with a tablecloth, three people, each holding a different drink</u>

The thud of grilled sardines being placed at the center of the table was the signal. The person holding tsipouro begins to sing loudly. The person holding beer waits a moment, finds the right timing, and begins to sing even louder. The person holding tea does not sing. Instead, he begins banging on the table.

Grilled squid is brought out. The table thuds again.
At that moment, the person holding tsipouro goes silent. He does not notice he has closed his mouth. The song continues inside his head. The distinction between whether the voice is going out or staying in has dissolved.

Then the person holding beer goes silent. Inside his head, a large sound reverberates. Following that, the person holding tea stops banging the table.

Because the sounds reverberating inside each other's heads have become audible to all three, they continue to play the music in silence. The beer person is also silent.

The tea person also stops his hands.

All three have their eyes open. All three are silent. The restaurant has gone quiet. But between the three, something is still sounding. It appears to be sounding. At least, the three believe so. Whether it is truly audible is outside the reach of this narration.

A certain workshop
37.96176838798409, 23.731607461644405
<u>A woman, a kite on the workshop table, the child is at the woman's mother's place</u>

The woman has a husband. An earnest, gentle husband. One day, this husband announced he would make a kite. For their unborn child, he said. From the woman's perspective, the husband was possessed by the very idea of "doing something" for the child. What he made, or why he made it, did not actually matter, she thought. Bamboo or paper, it made no difference. It did not have to be a kite. He simply needed the fact of his hands moving. The fact that he had already begun something for the thing about to be born. That is what put the husband at ease.

The woman does not blame him for this. She was only a little lonely. What the husband was looking at was the child, the kite for the child, and himself making the kite. The woman was not inside that triangle.

The woman looks at the kite on the table. And secretly, on its back, she writes in pencil, faintly, the names of the husband, herself, and the child.

When this kite rises into the sky, the names the woman wrote will be exposed to the eyes of everyone looking up from below. Whether they can read them or not, the names are there.

The husband will again look at the starry sky on the front and be satisfied. He will not care what is written on the back.

The woman knows this.
