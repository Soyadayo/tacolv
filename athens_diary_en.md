As a Japanese person, my palate whispered to me that the fish roe cream would probably go well with rice too.

After finishing some writing, I headed for a hill where people were reportedly flying kites. Vasillis had told me about this tradition. I walked to Philopappos Hill. Following Google's directions, I found myself on some narrow, unfamiliar path climbing uphill, but I went along with it. I could already see the kites, though I had no idea how to get there from where I was. I've been training my legs and core since last October, and the fact that I wasn't even out of breath on this steep incline proved it was paying off. When I came here last May, I remember the climb being unbearably hard.

My mind turned to the kite festival back home in Japan. In my hometown, it's the biggest event of the year, spanning three days. Neighborhoods fly kites roughly ten square meters in size, then slam them into each other to cut the opponents' strings — a somewhat rough affair. The kites are made to celebrate the birth of a child, so every neighborhood fights fiercely to win for that child's sake. It was always my favorite festival of the year. While I was lost in these thoughts, I reached the top of the hill. Google had brought me right to the spot. I scrambled up the slippery rocks, and at the summit, there were crowds of people — some flying kites, others watching. Fewer than ten kites were actually in the air, but the place was packed. I found a quieter spot and sat on the smooth rock. My mind went blank. I just enjoyed being there.
